Dear Editor,

Please consider the research article "Moisture Provenance and Counterfactual Attribution of Transboundary Atmospheric Water Effects: A Modelling Framework with Offshore Wind-Farm Experiments" for Environmental Modelling & Software.

The paper defines transboundary atmospheric water effects as counterfactual precipitation differences and separates three evidence classes: moisture provenance accounting from an open, mass-conserving tagging tool; a supply-channel scenario; and model-conditional contrasts from paired WRF simulations with and without a wind-farm parameterisation, each accompanied by a no-wind initial-condition ensemble. Known-truth experiments, placebo sources and a detectability analysis test what each class can identify. Applied to European offshore wind farms with ERA5 (2016–2021) and 3 prespecified WRF events, accounting is strongly transboundary, near-farm wind and boundary-layer responses are clear, and cross-border precipitation contrasts are not distinguishable from internal variability. The WRF results are reported as model-conditional contrasts, not as observed real-world effects.

The work fits the journal's scope on model development, evaluation and identification. The manuscript has 2,226 words in the main text, 119 words in the abstract, 11 figures, 1 table and 27 references. Software is openly available (see the Software and data availability section). The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.
